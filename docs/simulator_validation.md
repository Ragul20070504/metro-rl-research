### Notes on the comparisons

- **End-to-end running time (one direction)** — published actual runtime median 37.2 min
- **Weekday round trips per day** — reference policy inducts at the rate that reproduces the published trip count
- **Minimum headway** — the simulator's constraint is set from this measurement, so agreement is by construction
- **Weekday boardings per day** — demand process is fitted to observed ridership, so this checks the sampler, not the level
- **Mean passenger platform wait** — Additional Platform Time measures EXCESS wait relative to schedule, whereas the simulator reports TOTAL wait. The published figure is therefore a lower bound on the simulated quantity, not a target. Reported for order-of-magnitude only.
- **Fraction of dispatched trains completing their trip** — the published metric counts trains run against trains scheduled; the simulated analogue counts completed round trips against inductions, which is close but not identical in definition
- **Train failures per operating day** — MDBF 124,133 car-miles over 24 months implies 0.331 failures/day, i.e. 1.66 expected across the 5 validation episodes (P[observe zero] = 0.19). The hazard is set FROM this rate, so the comparison checks the implementation, not the rate; and at this sample size it could not distinguish a correct rate from one wrong by a factor of two. The aggregate rate is calibrated to real data; the within-day timing structure remains synthetic (assumption A13).

## Quantities that CANNOT be validated

Research brief section 11 requires these to be stated explicitly rather than passed over. Each is a limitation of the study, not an oversight.

| Quantity | Why no validation is possible |
|---|---|
| Train capacity / peak occupancy | no public occupancy data exists for this system; capacity is assumption A09 and is swept |
| Station dwell-time distribution | GTFS reports arrival == departure at intermediate stops, so it carries no dwell information at all; assumption A08, swept |
| Passenger waiting behaviour (balking, patience) | no public data; infinite patience is assumed by default and a finite-patience variant is swept (assumption A06) |
| Origin-destination distribution | the demand source publishes entries only; the OD model is fabricated (assumption A03) and three variants are swept in EXP-SENS-002 |
| Per-train trajectories and delays | GTFS-RT has no public historical archive; only aggregate realised statistics exist |
| Energy consumption in physical units | no citable kWh/train-km figure sourced (assumption A15); energy is reported in train-km-equivalent units and never in kWh |
| Operating cost in currency | no citable cost figure sourced (assumption A16); cost is reported in induction-equivalent units and never in currency |

**The two most consequential unvalidatable quantities — peak occupancy and the origin-destination distribution — both feed the pre-registered primary endpoint.** That is why the OD sweep (`EXP-SENS-002`) and the capacity sweep are mandatory rather than optional, and why any primary conclusion must either hold across the sweep or be reported as parameter-dependent.
